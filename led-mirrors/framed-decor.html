Mirrors as decoration, not just for checking your hair

A well-chosen wall mirror does three jobs at once. It reflects light, so a dark room feels brighter. It doubles the sense of space, which helps in smaller apartments and narrow corridors. And it becomes a piece of decor in its own right, like a painting that changes with the room. That's why framed and decorative mirrors are one of the easiest ways to finish a room.

Framed mirrors without lights

If a room already has good light, a framed mirror gives you a clean, finished look without wiring.

  • Framed Rectangle Mirror: a brushed aluminium frame in matt black, gold, rose gold or silver, from 24 x 36 inches.
  • Round Framed Mirror: a fine metal ring frame in five finishes.
  • Oval Framed Mirror: a softer shape for entrances and bedrooms.
  • Arch Window Framed Mirror: an arched frame inspired by window panes.

A group of small mirrors can fill a large wall more cheaply than one big piece. Our Decorative Wall Mirror Set comes in sets of 2, 4 or 6 and suits stairways, lounges and entrances.

Mirror wall design ideas

  • Entrance: a round or arch mirror above a console table gives guests a last look before they leave and makes a small entrance feel bigger.
  • Living room: a large mirror opposite a window brings in daylight. Above a sofa, choose a mirror about two-thirds the width of the sofa.
  • Bedroom: a framed full length mirror beside the wardrobe, or a round mirror above the dressing table.
  • Dining room: a wide mirror on the long wall makes the room feel larger and reflects the dining light in the evening.
  • Stairway: a group of small decorative mirrors climbing with the stairs.

Choosing a frame finish

Match the frame to the metal already in the room. Gold and champagne suit warm interiors with wood and beige tones. Matt black works with modern black taps, handles and lights. Silver and rose gold suit lighter, softer rooms. If the room has no strong metal finish, black is the safest choice.

How to hang a wall mirror correctly

Hang a wall mirror so its centre is around eye level, about 57 to 65 inches from the floor. Above furniture, leave 6 to 10 inches between the furniture and the bottom of the mirror. Heavy mirrors need proper wall plugs and brackets, not adhesive strips. On brick and concrete walls, we use heavy-duty fixings; our team installs mirrors free in Lahore.

Where not to place a mirror

Avoid placing a mirror where it reflects something you don't want to see twice, such as a cluttered corner or a bathroom door. Many families also prefer not to place a mirror directly facing the bed or the main door, and instead use a side wall. Opposite or beside a window is usually the best spot, because the mirror reflects daylight deeper into the room.

Mixing mirrors with other decor

Mirrors work well with wall lights on either side, a console with a lamp, or a group of framed pictures. When grouping mirrors and pictures, keep the spacing equal and align either the centres or the top edges. Mix shapes (round, arch, oval) for a relaxed look, or repeat one shape for a more formal one.
